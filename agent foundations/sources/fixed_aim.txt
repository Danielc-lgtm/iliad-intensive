Optimizer as:
- Steering reality towards narrow regions of configuration, an optimizer causes the environment to have a convergent attractor (which represents target states)
- Borrow intuitions from control theory
- Optimizer can start with a wide variety of initial conditions of the environment, face a wide range of perturbations, and still end up with narrow set of final condition in which its goals are "achieved"
- Entropy reduction: If you have high uncertainty about the initial condition of a subsystem S, but you know some agent is trying to optimize the subsystem and steer it to particular configuration, then you will have low uncertainty/entropy about final states of the system as you know the agent will overcome obstacles to achieve that state
-  Why care about optimizers: Suppose that you're an agent in the universe who just cares about prediction, if there is an optimizer out there, then merely for the purpose of prediction, it's useful for you to pay attention that optimizer (the preferences of that optimizer, or what outcomes the optimizer is trying to steer towards), because learning about the optimizer allows you to predict the future of the system (you will predict that the system will converge to the state that the optimizer is trying to steer towards), and you can make predictions just by learning about the optimizer preferences, without having to know the initial conditions of the system itself (since a wide variety of initial conditions will be funneled to the same narrow set of final conditions). Paying attention to the optimizer is efficient for prediction in an information theoretic sense (learning a small amount of info in the present (existence/preference of the optimizer) allows you to predict a large amount of info on the future.
- (Contrast the above with the usual case for prediction, where you need to know the precise initial conditions to be able to predict a system well, and even then it's still hard to predict the final state, learning about the preference of an optimizer is like the opposite of that)
- There's a view that "intelligence" is "observer-dependent", that e.g. we think of humans as intelligent because they perform well on certain tasks we care about, but those tasks themselves and the criteria to judge performance is set by us and relative to our subjective standards, so with a different set of tasks humans won't be intelligent (search online for this kind of view, steelman it and present it better). Our thermodynamic/information theoretic perspective is a counter to that perspective: Without making assumptions about what your goals are (supposing that you only care about predictions), there is an information theoretic reason for you to care about optimizers in the universe (which is not relative to your particular subjective standards as an observer).
