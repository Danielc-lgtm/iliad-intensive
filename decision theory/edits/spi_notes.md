- Pareto-frontier shaped renegotiation sets incentivizes exploitation:
  
  - Pareto frontier="Making my opponent better off implies making me worse off and vice versa"
  
  - Including the pareto-frontier in renegotiation set-> Opponent can make herself better off by making me worse off (exploitation)
  
  - Think about the point A on the pareto-frontier that's best for me and worst for my opponent (there's no way to make myself further better off by making opponent worse off)
  
  - Reducing my opponent's utility below point A is not even beneficial for myself by definition (so I have incentive to include points up to point A in my renegotiation set)
  
  - On the other hand, including points beyond point A means that exploitation becomes possible (opponent can make herself better off by making me worse off)
  
  - Hence we have guarantees on PMM

- Why entanglement-free SPIs can't guarantee PMM
  
  - One can use the renegotiation program to incentivize the opponent to submit default programs that are more cooperative
  
  - E.g. encode the rule "If you submit programs that are more cooperative, I will offer even more pareto-improvements and vice versa" (create incentive gradient)
  
  - Conversely, an renegotiation program may offer less pareto-improvement for default programs not as cooperative to incentivize cooperation
  
  - Hence we lose the tight PMM bound
